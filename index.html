Research

Working Papers

Priority Design for Grant-Funding Policies

Revise & Resubmit, Games and Economic Behavior

Abstract

Grant-making institutions seek to reward merit while guaranteeing support for designated priorities, but unlike seats or slots, grants differ in cost and may be partially funded. We extend the priority-design approach from the allocation of indivisible objects to the division of a fixed pool of funds among such grants. We study two ways of pursuing these priorities: earmarking funds for each category, or setting category-specific floors. With earmarks, we characterize the unique rule that is non-wasteful, respects merit priorities, and splits the cost of a grant eligible for several categories equally among them wherever their reserves allow. With floors, which cannot always be met exactly, we identify the appropriate relaxation and construct a simple rule that satisfies it; under that rule, a grant cannot turn a rejection into an award by requesting more funding at the same merit, and the allocation is unaffected by grants that were never in contention for funding. By contrast, when restricted to integer problems, the rule that funds the highest-merit grants as generously as the constraints allow loses both properties, so efficiency comes at the cost of these guarantees.
